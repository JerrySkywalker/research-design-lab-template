# Prior-art research workflow

This is a lightweight research procedure, not a claim that every database or corpus is available.

1. Define the research question, intended use, time/cutoff assumptions, and exclusion criteria in a research log entry.
2. Record a search strategy: concepts/synonyms, source classes, planned databases or repositories, query/date range, and access limitations. Source classes normally include literature, standards, and patents; add other classes only when relevant.
3. Screen candidates in the source register. Record locator, class, access status, include/exclude/pending outcome, and a short relevance/limitation note. Mark unavailable or partial sources explicitly.
4. Build a compact novelty/collision matrix in a synthesis: rows are candidate claims or questions, columns are screened sources, and cells say supported, conflicting, adjacent, or unknown with source IDs. A matrix is an aid to analysis, not legal advice or a finding of novelty.
5. Record evidence gaps, unresolved collisions, and research implications. Link implications to hypotheses or admitted research only when supported; do not select a design.
6. Synthesize conclusions with claim labels, provenance, limits, and a recommendation if useful. Owner adoption still requires an explicit decision record.
